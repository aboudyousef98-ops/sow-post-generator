# 03 — Legal and Compliance Checklist

Not legal advice. Use it to brief a lawyer and to keep marketing truthful.

## 1. Employer conflict (do this first)
The founder currently does solar design work for an employer. Before launch:
- [ ] Read the employment contract for non-compete, non-solicit, moonlighting and IP-assignment clauses.
- [ ] Work produced for the employer (plan sets, calcs, templates, AHJ databases, tools) normally belongs to the employer. Do not reuse it, even anonymized, without written permission.
- [ ] Do not use employer accounts, repos, licences, client lists or working hours for this company.
- [ ] Keep this plan and the website in a separate personal repo.
- [ ] Portfolio "past work" = only work you own or have written permission to show. Otherwise recreate similar designs from scratch as labeled concept projects.

## 2. Entity
- [ ] Form an LLC (state of residence or of main operations); get EIN; open a business bank account.
- [ ] If the founder is outside the US: check registered-agent, tax and banking requirements with an accountant.
- [ ] Professional liability (E&O) insurance before the first paid set.

## 3. Engineering licensure
- "Engineer"/"engineering" are protected terms in most states; offering engineering services usually needs a licensed PE and often a firm registration (Certificate of Authorization). Rules differ by state: verify CA, TX, FL, AZ individually with each board.
- [ ] Company name and descriptor avoid "Engineering" until this is in place.
- [ ] Partner PE must be in responsible charge of what they stamp, under their state's rules; "plan stamping" without real review is prohibited. The agreement should define review scope, liability, insurance, fee and turnaround.
- [ ] Website wording: "PE-stamped through licensed partner engineers in [list of states with signed partners]".
- [ ] No contractor work (installation) is offered, so no contractor licence claims.

## 4. Marketing truthfulness (FTC, platform rules)
- [ ] No invented clients, testimonials, project counts, team size or offices.
- [ ] Concept projects labeled; AI imagery not presented as real sites.
- [ ] Job posts only for real roles; talent-network posts say they are a roster.
- [ ] Follow LinkedIn and Instagram terms on automation and authenticity; label AI-generated media where the platform asks.

## 5. Website must-haves
- [ ] Privacy policy, terms, cookie notice if analytics is used.
- [ ] Business contact details; no fake street address (a registered-agent or virtual address is fine if disclosed as mailing address).
- [ ] Accessibility: WCAG 2.1 AA target.

## 6. Email
- [ ] CAN-SPAM for any outreach: real sender, physical mailing address, unsubscribe.
